# Two-gang switch 67181844 pairing investigation

## Observed failure

On 2026-11-07 the owner reported that Home Assistant's single-button pairing
detected switch `67181844`, physically identified as a two-gang switch, but did
not add it. The displayed observation said its pairing signature was
unrecognized; the form also showed `Reason: None` and duplicated punctuation.

The HA specialist inspected existing status, the durable command record and
available logs on the HA gateway without issuing radio commands or changing
the network. The reported deployment was app 0.5.0 / radio 0.13. At
15:08:55 UTC (16:08:55 Europe/Warsaw), the retained session was:

- Session: `b6ff4033-31ac-4e5e-829b-d2c1e75e1d93`.
- Terminal status: `expired`; `registered: false`.
- Session error: `unsupported_device_seen`.
- Observation: device `67181844`, suffix `0002`, reason
  `unknown_pairing_payload`.
- The device was absent from the daemon registry.

The 120-second commissioning command's durable result was updated at
15:06:59.331939 UTC. The last retained discovery timestamp was
15:06:13.153332 UTC. These are recorded result/discovery times, not a recovered
packet capture or proof of the exact time the owner viewed the form.

## Confirmed cause and evidence limit

The classifier recognized the subject suffix but rejected the registration
payload. Its existing two-gang rule requires suffix `0002`, payload prefix
`00 02 03 05 34 01`, and final byte `00`; byte 6 may vary. A native request must
also pass CRC decoding and the existing source/subject, tag and destination
checks before producing this observation.

The exact rejected payload was not retained in the public session record or
available logs. The ZigBee specialist scanned 64 existing local pcapng files
without read errors and found no native pairing request from this device.
Historical control/announcement traffic corroborates suffix `0002`, but does
not establish its registration signature. The differing byte(s), their meaning
and a safe broader matching rule therefore remain unknown.

There is no evidence here that the switch is one-gang or that it cannot be
controlled. There is also no justification for accepting every `0002` payload,
hardcoding this device ID, or changing radio firmware.

## Display issue and follow-up

The HA form used the literal fallback `None` when no session-level error was
present. That can occur while a session is still listening, even when a device
observation already explains why it is ineligible. The precise earlier screen
state was not retained, so this explains a rendering path rather than proving
the owner saw a particular intermediate snapshot. Observation text and the
form template also both supplied terminal punctuation.

The remediation separates clearer HA session/observation wording from bounded
retention of checksum-validated pairing payload evidence during an explicit
commissioning session. Each retained observation keeps at most two distinct
signatures with their exact eight-byte payloads; a flag reports additional
variants. Known counter-only changes do not cause extra persistence writes.
Evidence survives terminal outcomes/restarts, but never restores admission
eligibility. Older records without evidence remain supported.

HA exposes this evidence in diagnostic attributes and an anonymous diagnostic
download, not in the main pairing form. Neither change relaxes admission or
automatically repeats pairing. A later operator-authorized attempt after
deployment can establish whether an additional profile is warranted.

Independent offline validation passed 63 daemon commissioning, capture,
profile, discovery and CLI tests, plus 57 HA commissioning, client, simulated
native pairing and integration tests. These cover both supported gang counts,
unknown/conflicting evidence, persistence, bounded fields, old records,
diagnostic redaction and the reported wording. Test-generated payload variants
are synthetic regressions, not recovered bytes from switch `67181844`.

No new pairing attempt, manual admission, output command, firmware update or
service restart was performed as part of this investigation. Physical pairing
success and recognition of this variant remain unverified.
